## I've always played a lot of games

<Video src="/notes/making-a-game/tft-grandmaster.mp4" width={1280} height={720} caption="The moment I reached Grandmaster in TFT" />

I've played all sorts of games for a long time, ever since I was a kid, and even now the first thing I do after work is turn on a game.

TFT Grandmaster, 1,500 hours of PUBG, all kinds of RPGs: I've spent my life playing games, and playing them hard.

## I work at a game company, but...

I work as a developer at a game company, yet I had never actually made a game. My whole career has been in blockchain, so I don't know much about game development.

Just thinking about it vaguely, the barrier looked far too high to get over alone: not only building the game itself, but illustration, 3D modeling, sound and more.

## The age of AI

<Video src="/notes/making-a-game/maple-survivor.mp4" width={960} height={772} caption="A MapleStory roguelike I made on my own" />

Times have changed a lot. Exaggerating a little, if you have an idea, you can make a game, and development that used to take ages has become far easier.

Not long ago I briefly worked on a project at work about making games with AI, and it showed me how much is possible now. Maybe I could make the game I want, too.

The video above is part of a MapleStory roguelike demo I made after work around that time.

## What I'm making now

<Video src="/notes/making-a-game/rcg2-tour.mp4" width={1280} height={720} caption="The card game I'm making now (all art and UI are placeholders for now)" />

These days I'm making a roguelike card game. (It's still very much a prototype, though.)
In a small 3D room, you draw cards, customize their stats, play matches, move through a scenario... I'm building whatever ideas come to me.

The goal: release a game on Steam. Whether it'll be this card game, I don't know yet.
